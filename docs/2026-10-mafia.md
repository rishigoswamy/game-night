# Mafia: rules for Game Night (October 2026)

This note records the rules of Game Night's Mafia (`public/games/party/mafia.js`) and the research behind them. The
game follows it; the unit tests (`test/unit/party-mafia.test.js`) check the rules below.

## The game

Mafia was created by Dimitry Davidoff at Moscow State University (1986–87). Werewolf is a re-theme of it, and
Game Night's One Night Werewolf is a one-round spin-off.

An informed minority, the **mafia**, who know each other, plays against the uninformed **town**. Each round has two
parts:
- **Night:** the mafia secretly kill, and roles with powers act.
- **Day:** everyone debates and votes someone out.

The town wins when the mafia are gone; the mafia win at **parity**.

In Game Night the **Mac is the moderator**, so everyone plays. Each phone holds its player's secret role and night
actions. The game is for **4–16 players** and has a 1920s noir theme: the narrator tells a short story for each death.

## A round

1. **Deal:**
   - Roles appear face down on the phones: tap to peek, tap again to hide. The card also closes by itself at every new
     phase, so a role is never left showing.
   - The mafia see their team, the Masons each other, and the Executioner their target.
   - The Mac lists the roles in the game, but not who has them.
2. **Night** (one timer, default 30 s): **every living player taps something**, so tapping gives nothing away.
   - Power roles act.
   - The mafia see each other's picks live.
   - Everyone else picks a private *hunch*.
3. **Dawn:** deaths are narrated and roles revealed (see Setup). Saves aren't announced. Private results go to the
   phones that earned them.
4. **Day:**
   - **Discussion:** 4 min on day 1, 1 min less each day, minimum 2. "Ready to vote" on every phone ends it early.
   - **Nominations** (60 s): the first player nominated by a majority of the living goes on trial. If time runs out,
     the player with the most nominations (at least 2) does. A tie at the top follows the tie setting.
   - **Defense** (30 s): the accused pleads aloud.
   - **Verdict** (20 s): Guilty / Innocent / Abstain. **More Guilty than Innocent eliminates; a tie acquits.** The Mac
     then shows who voted what.
   - An acquittal returns to nominations, up to the day's trial limit (default 2).
5. **Eliminated players** become spectators: their phone shows every role and the night live. They stay silent.

## Setup options

| Option | Choices (default first) |
|---|---|
| Roles on death | Revealed · Hidden until the end |
| Nomination ties | Nobody goes on trial · One of the tied at random |
| Mafia kill | Live votes, the leader breaks ties · Unanimous · The leader decides |
| First night | A kill · The mafia only meet |
| Number of mafia | Suggested `round(players / 4)`, at least 1 (4–5 → 1, 6–9 → 2, 10–13 → 3, 14–16 → 4); the host can change it by one either way |
| Doctor | Self-save once a game · never · any time; can't protect the same player two nights running (on · off) |
| Times | Day-1 discussion, shrinking on/off, trials per day (1–3), night length (20–60 s) |
| Role limits | Vigilante shots (2), Veteran alerts (2), Janitor cleans (2), Survivor vests (2) |

## Roles

An action aimed at a player is a **visit** (seen by the Lookout, Tracker, Veteran and Bodyguard). A blocked player
doesn't visit.

| Level | Town | Mafia | Neutral |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beginner** | Villager, **Detective** (Suspicious / Not suspicious), **Doctor** (protects one player) | **Mafioso** | |
| **Intermediate** | **Escort** (blocks), **Bodyguard** (dies instead, takes the attacker), **Vigilante** (limited shots, guilt if they shoot town), **Masons ×2** (know each other; 7+ players), **Mayor** (reveals by day; then votes count 3, can't be healed), **Lookout** (who visited X), **Tracker** (whom X visited), **Medium** (once, learns a dead player's role; hidden mode only) | **Godfather** (looks Not suspicious; leads the kill), **Consort** (blocks) | **Jester** (wins if voted out at a trial), **Survivor** (wins if alive; vests) |
| **Advanced** | **Veteran** (on alert, kills every visitor) | **Framer** (target looks Suspicious tonight), **Janitor** (hides the victim's role; reveal mode only), **Consigliere** (learns an exact role) | **Executioner** (wins if their target is voted out; becomes a Jester if the target dies at night), **Serial Killer** (kills nightly, looks Suspicious, kills whoever blocks them) |

**The Detective's answers:**
- *Suspicious:* any mafia except the Godfather, a framed player, the Serial Killer.
- *Not suspicious:* everyone else.

**The mafia's kill** is carried out by one of the mafia who picked the target, chosen at random (by the leader, when
the leader decides). If that one is blocked, another who picked it takes over.

**Limits:**
- at most one Serial Killer;
- neutrals at most `floor(players / 5)`;
- roles that don't fit are left out, most advanced first.

## Night order

Actions resolve in steps, simultaneously within a step. A player killed tonight still acts.

1. Veteran alert
2. Blocks (Escort, Consort; a block on the Serial Killer fails and makes the blocker their target)
3. The mafia's kill decided
4. Protection and deception (Doctor, Bodyguard, Survivor vest, Framer)
5. Attacks (mafia, Vigilante, Serial Killer, Veteran). A heal or vest stops every attack on that player. The Bodyguard
   takes the first attack on the guarded player, and the attacker dies too.
6. Information (Detective, Consigliere, Lookout, Tracker)
7. Afterwards: Janitor, Executioner → Jester, Vigilante guilt, use counters

The Medium's look is answered at dawn.

## Winning

| Side | Wins when |
|---|---|
| Town | No mafia and no Serial Killer alive |
| Mafia | Mafia equal or outnumber everyone else alive, and the Serial Killer is dead |
| Serial Killer | Alive with at most one other player (Survivors, Jesters and Executioners don't count) |
| Survivor | Alive at the end, whoever else wins |
| Jester, Executioner | At their trial; the game goes on |

The game is a **draw** if everyone left dies the same night, or after 3 rounds in a row without a death.

**On the leaderboard,** every player on the winning side gets a win, dead or alive; neutrals win by their own goal.

## Why these rules

- **Disputed rules became setup options:** reveal on death, ties, the mafia kill, the first night, the Doctor's
  limits. Sources disagree on each, and groups have their own habits.
- **The trial** (nominate → defense → verdict) comes from Town of Salem: the most dramatic format, and one a narrating
  Mac can run. A tied verdict acquits, as there.
- **Mafia count:** sources range from Davidoff's ⅓ to the modern 1 in 4–5. Theory (Braverman, Etesami & Mossel) says
  the mafia grow like √players, but live play favours the town more than theory predicts. One in four sits between
  them.
- **The everyone-taps night** replaces role-by-role calling. With phones in every hand, the sound of tapping would
  otherwise reveal the power roles.
- **Left out:**
  - **Silencer, Blackmailer:** "don't speak tomorrow" only works on trust, the reason the Werewolf Curator was left
    out.
  - **Chat and last wills:** the game has no phone chat, so the Medium is the chat-free version.
  - **Jailor, Transporter, Witch, Amnesiac, Arsonist:** each adds a mechanic of its own; possible later.
